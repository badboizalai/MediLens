# Elicitation Techniques (solo-friendly)

## 1. Interview script (30 minutes, 5–8 people who have been patients or caregivers)
1. Warm-up: last time you or a relative received a medical record — what happened next?
2. Understanding: what parts were hard to understand? Show me (redacted) if possible.
3. Visualisation: if you could see it on a body picture, what would you want to see first?
4. Change over time: how do you track whether treatment worked?
5. Trust: what would make you afraid to upload a record? What would make you trust the app?
6. Sharing: who would you share with, for how long?
7. Close: anything we did not ask that matters?
Record: quotes (anonymised), pains, gains, surprises. Never record identifiable health details.

## 2. Process mapping
- As-Is: patient receives record → reads → confused → searches internet → asks doctor (limited time).
- To-Be: upload → review → viewer → explanation → questions list for doctor → share.
- Notation: numbered steps or BPMN (pools: Patient, MediLens, AI worker, Clinician).

## 3. Self-workshop formats (playing all stakeholders)
- **Six hats** for a feature: facts, feelings, risks, benefits, ideas, process — 5 minutes each.
- **Pre-mortem**: "It is 6 months later and the feature failed. Why?" → becomes risks/abuse cases.
- **Example mapping** per story: rules (blue), examples (green), questions (red) → AC.

## 4. Document analysis
Collect redacted samples of each document type; record layout zones (header, patient info, diagnosis, tables, signature/stamp) — these feed AI schemas and test data.

## 5. Regulation scan
For every feature ask: consent? purpose limitation? minimum data? retention? cross-border transfer? data subject rights? → add requirements accordingly.
